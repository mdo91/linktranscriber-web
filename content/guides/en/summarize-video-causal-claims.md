To summarize a video's explanation of cause and effect, create a built-in Damas summary, then check whether the speaker describes an observation, proposes a cause or presents evidence for that cause. Retain their uncertainty and any alternatives in your own short account.

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use material you have permission to process. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported; public links can also be inaccessible. Damas is available on compatible iPhone, iPad and Mac devices.

## Create and review the summary

1. Keep the original link and title. Connect to the internet, sign in with Apple and provide the supported link in Damas. Review the AI processing consent. Processing uses media minutes and Insight credits; review balances, usage history and the cost before confirmation. Current plans and optional top-ups appear in the app.
2. Create an original-language transcript. Open the completed, nonempty transcript from Library and select the wand-and-stars Insight Studio control. Choose a short summary in Standard style and select your output language. Supply the causal question as topic or context, for example: “What explanation does the speaker offer for the change, and what limits do they mention?”
3. Review the displayed Insight cost and generate the summary. The original transcript remains separate from the saved result in Outputs. Processing can continue when you leave the app; a completion notification is available when enabled.
4. Read the summary and any source excerpt, then replay the relevant explanation in the public recording. Check names, quantities, negatives and qualifications. Inspect charts or demonstrations yourself when they are not described aloud. If the overview omits an important qualification, create another summary with more specific context and check it again.

## Separate the change from its explanation

In your own note, record four things: what changed or was observed; the cause the speaker proposes; evidence they give for the proposed link; and alternatives or limits they mention. Attach a checked passage to each. If the speaker merely says two things occurred together, write that association without turning it into a cause. If they say “may have caused,” preserve that uncertainty.

A sequence is also not enough on its own: an event following another may have a different explanation. Ask whether the recording describes a comparison, an intervention, or only a sequence. Record the conditions and any competing explanation the speaker actually discusses. If a comparison or alternative is absent, mark it as a question for further investigation, not as evidence that it was ruled out. This worksheet is your manual review, not an automatic Damas fact check.

## Fictional example: attendance after a reminder

Imagine a speaker says attendance was higher after a reminder was sent, but also notes that the second event was held at a different time. This is an invented teaching example, not a quotation or a test of Damas.

| Record | What the example supports |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | Higher attendance followed the reminder. |
| Proposed cause | The reminder may have helped, if the speaker says so. |
| Alternative | The different event time could matter; the example does not measure its effect. |
| Next check | Look for a comparable event or evidence addressing the different time. |

A careful summary says that attendance rose after the reminder and that the change of time leaves the reason uncertain. It does not say the reminder caused the increase. If a real source supplies a comparison, record its conditions and limits before making a stronger statement.

## Finish the note

Keep the source link, checked passages and unresolved questions with your summary. Use quotation marks only for exact words checked in the recording; label your shorter account as a paraphrase. A faithful account of a speaker's explanation does not independently establish the cause. If audio or access is insufficient, leave the check open.
