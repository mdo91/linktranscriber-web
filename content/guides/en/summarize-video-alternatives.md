To summarize a video comparing two alternatives, create a transcript and built-in summary in Damas, then make a manual table of each alternative against the criteria the speaker actually discusses. Keep conditions and missing comparisons visible; do not declare an overall winner unless the source supports that conclusion.

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use material you have permission to process. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported, and public links can also be inaccessible. Damas is available on compatible iPhone, iPad and Mac devices.

## Prepare the comparison

1. Retain the original link and title. Note which alternatives the speaker compares and the question you want to answer.
2. Connect to the internet, sign in with Apple and provide the link in Damas. Review AI processing consent. Processing uses media minutes and Insight credits; check balances, usage history and cost before confirming. Current plans and optional top-up packs appear in the app.
3. Follow the app's transcription and summary instructions. Choose a short or detailed Standard or Professional summary, adding your topic or context and preferred output language if useful. Keep the original transcript and save the AI output separately.
4. Processing can continue when you leave the app, with a completion notification when enabled. Read the overview, then replay the full comparison and inspect any visual demonstration yourself. Check numbers, negatives and qualifications before editing or copying text.

The [general summary guide](/guides/summarize-video-transcript-iphone/) explains the starting workflow. A fluent overview can omit the conditions under which a comparison was made.

## Make a criterion-by-criterion record

For each criterion, write the speaker's observation about each alternative, the conditions, and the checked passage. If only one alternative is discussed, mark the other “not compared” rather than assuming it has the opposite property. Keep a recommendation for a particular situation separate from a claim that one choice is best for everyone. The table is your manual worksheet, not an automatic Damas evaluation.

Compare the same measure under the same conditions before describing a difference. If the recording changes the setting or gives no comparable result, write “comparison unresolved”. Record a speaker's preference as their preference, and retain any qualification they give. A comparison of two examples does not establish how every other example behaves.

## Fictional example: two workshop layouts

Imagine a presenter compares two room layouts for a small workshop. They say layout A gives each participant more table space in the demonstrated room, while layout B leaves a wider central aisle. They do not compare setup time. This invented example is not a quotation or an app test.

| Criterion | A | B | What the note can say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Table space | More in the demonstrated room | Less in that comparison | A has more table space under the demonstrated conditions. |
| Central aisle | Narrower in that comparison | Wider | B leaves a wider aisle in that room. |
| Setup time | Not compared | Not compared | No speed conclusion is supported. |

A useful summary says that A and B have different stated advantages in the demonstrated room. It does not say B is faster to arrange or that A is the best layout for every event. If your own priority is aisle width, label that as your decision criterion, not the presenter's conclusion.

## Copy the result with its evidence

Keep the link, checked passages, conditions and unresolved comparisons with your summary. Use quotation marks only for exact speech verified in the recording; label shortened wording as a paraphrase. A faithful report of a comparison does not independently prove its claims. If the source becomes inaccessible or a key passage is unclear, leave the check open. Share only within your reuse rights.
