To distinguish similar concepts in a lecture, create a Quiz from its transcript in Damas Insight Studio. Answer each question, read its explanation, then compare the answer choices with the lecturer's actual distinctions. Treat a plausible but unsupported choice as a reason to review the source, not as a fact to memorize.

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use material you have permission to process. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported, and even a public source may be inaccessible. Damas is available on compatible iPhone, iPad and Mac devices.

## Create the quiz

1. Keep the lecture link and title. Choose the two concepts you want to tell apart.
2. Connect to the internet, sign in with Apple and provide the supported link in Damas. Review the AI processing consent. Processing uses media minutes and Insight credits; check balances, usage history and cost before confirming. Current plans and optional top-ups are shown in the app.
3. Create an original-language transcript. Replay the relevant definitions, examples and conditions; inspect information shown only on screen yourself.
4. Open the completed, nonempty transcript from Library and select the wand-and-stars **Insight Studio** control. Under **Study**, choose **Quiz**, select an output language and use topic or context guidance to focus on the concepts you need to compare.
5. Review the displayed Insight cost and generate the quiz. Processing can continue while you leave the app; a completion notification is available when enabled. Open the saved result under **Outputs**. The original transcript remains separate.
6. Answer a question and read its explanation. Check important statements against the transcript, any Source excerpt and the original recording; a correct indication is not proof that the explanation is accurate. If useful, create another output with more specific topic or context guidance and check that result too.

The [lecture study guide](/guides/youtube-lecture-study-guide/) explains how to build a broader learning guide and use quiz feedback. Here the next task is to examine why alternatives differ.

## Inspect the choices yourself

For each choice, record the concept it describes, the condition that makes it fit or fail the question, and the checked passage that supports your decision. If the source does not provide enough information to decide, mark the choice unresolved. A choice can be true in another situation yet wrong for the question's stated conditions. If two choices both seem supported, check the wording and explanation before accepting the question as unambiguous.

This is your own review note, not an automatic grading or fact-checking feature. Keep the question, your reasoning, source link and unresolved points together. Do not manufacture a distinction merely to make one answer fit.

## Fictional example: a count and a share

Imagine a lecture defines a count as the number of attendees and a share as attendees divided by invitees. In a fictional question asking for the share, one choice gives 20 attendees and another gives 20 out of 100 invitees. The first is a count; the second supplies a numerator and denominator, so the share is 20%. A bare count does not establish that percentage without the invitee total. This is a teaching example, not a quotation or an app test.

In your own quiz, check whether the lecturer actually defined both terms and whether the question provides the necessary denominator. If it does not, leave the answer unresolved and revisit that section instead of learning a guessed number. Check the explanation as carefully as the choice.

## Decide what to revise

If you chose the wrong concept, revisit the lecture's definitions and make one new example of your own, clearly labeled as yours. If the quiz omits a needed condition or its explanation conflicts with the source, retain the checked source passage and revise your notes rather than memorizing the generated claim. If the audio is unclear, leave that point open. Use the [definition exercise](/guides/lecture-definition-boundaries/) to test conditions against cases, and keep the original link with your notes.
