To quote a numerical comparison from a video interview, use an editable Damas transcript to locate the figures, then check the spoken values and what each value measures. Record the group, period and denominator before comparing them. Keep any calculation you make outside the quotation: a faithfully copied number can still support a misleading comparison.

## Give each figure its own comparison record

For each figure, record the exact checked wording, quantity, unit, group counted, period and denominator if it is a rate or share. Put the supporting passage beside that record. If the denominator is absent, write “not stated” and do not infer it from a nearby number. Check that both figures use the same definition before describing an increase or decrease.

Ask two separate questions: did the count change, and did the share change? A larger count does not by itself establish a larger share. If the groups, periods or definitions differ, preserve those differences in the note; arithmetic alone cannot make them comparable. An approximate value stays approximate. Do not silently turn “up to”, “about” or a range into an exact result.

## Fictional example: more attendees, a smaller share

This is an invented teaching example, not a real interview, quotation or Damas calculation. Suppose a speaker describes two workshops: 20 attendees from 100 invitees at the first and 30 from 200 at the second, using the same definition of attendance.

- Count: 20 to 30 means 10 more attendees.
- Your calculation: 20 ÷ 100 = 20%; 30 ÷ 200 = 15%.
- Difference in shares: 15% − 20% = −5 percentage points. That is different from a decrease of 5%.
- Permitted paraphrase for this fictional example: the second workshop had more attendees but a lower attendance share among invitees.

Label the arithmetic as your calculation, not words spoken in the source. The figures alone do not explain why attendance changed or prove the workshops were otherwise comparable. If the second invitation count were missing, the second share would remain unknown. Do not manufacture that missing total to finish the table.

## Copy the comparison with its limits

Before sharing, check: are counts and shares labeled; is each denominator supported; are the periods and groups explicit; are your calculations outside the quotation; and does the conclusion say only what those records establish? Retain the original link, both checked passages and unresolved questions. Confirming what a speaker said does not independently verify their underlying data.
