To turn a video into a useful topic mind map, create its transcript in Damas, choose the built-in editable mind map and add your reading goal as context. Organize the result around questions you want to find again, then check branch labels and source passages. Keep repeated explanations under one topic with separate passage notes, rather than copying a branch every time the speaker returns to it.

On iPhone, use iOS 18.6 or later and an internet connection. Sign in with Apple for online processing and purchases, and review the AI processing consent. Processing uses media minutes and Insight credits: check balances, usage history and the cost before confirming. Available plans and optional top-up packs are shown in the app.

## Choose what the map should help you find

Use one source and one purpose, such as finding the explanations needed before taking a portrait. A topic map is useful when the speaker revisits the same subject in different parts of a recording. Its branches describe where information belongs; a branch does not establish that one statement causes or proves another.

1. Retain the original link and title. Paste an eligible link into Damas and follow the current transcription instructions.
2. Read the complete transcript, checking names, terms, numbers and qualifications against the recording. Inspect information shown only on screen yourself and label it separately.
3. Choose the editable mind map in Damas. Supply a topic, audience or context and select the output language. No external LLM is required.
4. Review the proposed hierarchy. Keep the original transcript and save the AI output separately. Edit the map where a label is too broad, duplicates another branch or introduces unsupported content.
5. In your own companion note, put a checked source passage and the surrounding words beside each useful topic. These are manual retrieval cues, not automatic timestamps or clickable video navigation.

## Use one organizing rule at each level

For the first level, choose reader questions of comparable scope. Under each, keep its explanation, example and any stated condition. Do not mix a tool, a step and an outcome as though they were three equivalent topics. Rename a vague label such as “other” to the question its contents actually answer.

When the same topic returns later, keep one main entry and record both passages in your companion note. If it genuinely belongs to two questions, add a written cross-reference rather than pretending the source presents two different explanations. Keep conflicting conditions visible instead of merging them.

## Fictional example: a portrait lesson

This is an invented teaching example, not a quotation, app test or photography recommendation. Suppose a lesson discusses light, framing and focus, then returns to light during a final demonstration.

| Reader question | Main branch | What stays in the companion note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does the speaker explain about light? | Light | Initial explanation and later demonstration, each with its own checked passage. |
| What is included in the frame? | Framing | Only the selection criteria actually stated. |
| Which part is discussed as the focus? | Focus | The explanation and any source-stated condition. |

Put the later light demonstration under the existing light topic. If it also illustrates framing, write “see the light demonstration” under framing and keep the original evidence in one place. Do not add camera settings that the speaker never states. The hierarchy helps locate explanations; it does not prove their technical correctness.

## Example context to copy into Damas

> Create a topic mind map for a reader who wants to find explanations again. Use one clear question per main branch. Group repeated discussion of the same topic without losing its separate conditions. Use only this source, do not add outside facts or quotations, and leave unclear material unresolved. Topic: [your purpose]. Output language: [language].

This is guidance, not a guarantee that the generated map follows the requested structure. Verify the result yourself. For every retained branch, ask whether its label matches its contents, whether you can find the supporting explanation again, and whether any important qualification disappeared. Remove unsupported branches; leave unclear audio pending.

Keep source link, title and unresolved questions with copied notes. A copied map label is your organizational wording, not a speaker quotation. Share only within your reuse rights.
