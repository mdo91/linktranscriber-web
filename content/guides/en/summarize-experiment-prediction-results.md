To summarize an experiment in a YouTube lecture, use a Damas transcript and built-in summary to locate the demonstration, then record the prediction, observation and explanation separately. Compare the result with the prediction before writing your short account. You make this experiment record manually; Damas supplies text you can read, edit and copy.

## Prepare the complete demonstration

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. On iPhone, iOS 18.6 or later, internet, Premium and consent for AI processing are required. Process only material you are authorized to use. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported; a public recording can also be inaccessible.

1. Keep the original link and title. Choose one experiment rather than the whole lecture.
2. Provide the supported link in Damas and follow the current transcription and built-in summary instructions, reviewing AI consent.
3. Read the overview, then replay the setup, prediction, trial and discussion in full. A preview of what should happen is not the outcome.
4. Check numbers, units, negatives and changes to the setup. Inspect any result shown only on screen yourself and label it as a visual observation.
5. Edit and copy the checked passages into your own note. Keep enough surrounding wording to find each passage again.

The [general summary workflow](/guides/summarize-video-transcript-iphone/) explains how to prepare the overview. The illustration shows the iPhone interface, not an experiment-analysis feature.

## Make a separate record for each trial

Use four fields for each trial: setup and conditions; prediction stated before the test; result actually reported or visible; explanation offered afterward. Attach a checked passage or labeled visual note to each field. A prediction without a reported observation leaves the result unknown, even if the lecturer sounds confident.

Compare like with like: the same outcome, unit and setup. Mark the prediction as met, not met or not assessable from the recording. Keep that judgment separate from whether one condition performed better than another. A better result can still fall short of the prediction. If materials or measurement conditions change, start another trial record rather than merging the figures.

Keep the lecturer’s explanation attributed to them. A visible difference does not, by itself, establish its cause. Do not supply an omitted mechanism from your own knowledge or treat a simulated result as a physical observation.

## Fictional example: a paper bridge

This invented teaching scenario is not a real experiment, source quotation or Damas test. Suppose a lecturer predicts that a folded paper bridge will hold eight counters. In the described trial, the flat bridge holds three and the folded bridge holds five before the stated stopping condition.

For this fictional record, both bridges use the same paper and span. The outcome is the largest number of counters held for ten seconds; keep that measurement rule with both counts.

| Field | Note for this fictional trial |
| --- | --- |
| Prediction | Folded bridge: eight counters |
| Reported observation | Flat: three; folded: five, with the trial’s stated stopping condition |
| Comparison | Folded holds more in this trial, but does not reach the prediction |
| Explanation | Record only the explanation the lecturer actually gives; do not invent one |

A useful short account retains both findings: the folded bridge holds more counters in the described trial, while the prediction of eight is not met. “The prediction was confirmed” would lose the important mismatch. If a later trial uses different paper, keep it separate. If the video ends before showing or reporting a result, write “result not available in this recording”, not eight.

## Write the summary from the record

Use this manual checklist: which trial; under what conditions; what was predicted; what was observed; whether the prediction was met; whose explanation is being reported; what remains unknown. Keep the original link with the final paragraph. Reserve quotation marks for exact wording checked against the recording. Never invent facts, quotations, owners or deadlines.

No external LLM is needed. Optional formatting in another LLM is a separate external step requiring permission to share the text and review of every output; it is not a Damas integration.
