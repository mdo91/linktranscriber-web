To turn a YouTube lecture into a study guide, transcribe an eligible public recording in Damas, choose its built-in study guide and give it your learning goal as context. Check the guide against the recording, then use its questions to identify what you need to revisit rather than treating a generated answer as an answer key.

Damas supports public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use only material you have permission to process. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported, and a public link can also be inaccessible. Damas is available on compatible iPhone, iPad and Mac devices.

## Prepare the lecture and the guide

1. Keep the lecture's original link and title. Write one learning goal, such as explaining a concept and distinguishing it from a similar one.
2. Connect to the internet, sign in with Apple and provide the supported link in Damas. Review the AI processing consent. Processing uses media minutes and Insight credits; check balances, usage history and the cost before confirming. Available plans and optional top-up packs appear in the app.
3. Follow the app's transcription instructions. Read the original-language transcript and replay important definitions, numbers and qualifications. Inspect diagrams and other screen-only information yourself.
4. Choose the built-in study guide, supply your topic, audience or context and select the output language. Keep the original transcript and save the AI output separately. Processing can continue after you leave the app; a completion notification is available when enabled.
5. Read the guide alongside the transcript and source. Edit or copy notes only after checking them; leave unclear audio or visual details unresolved.

## Make the guide useful for a review session

Divide your own review into three passes. First, identify the central idea and its stated conditions. Second, hide the explanation and try to answer a question in your own words. Third, compare your answer with the full checked passage and write what you missed. Do not mark an answer correct merely because it sounds plausible or matches a fluent generated explanation.

Keep a small manual record for each question: the question, your answer, the source passage, and the next review needed. If the lecture does not explain a point, mark it “not stated” and consult an appropriate additional source rather than filling the gap from the generated text. Damas can also create quizzes with explained answers and question-and-answer flashcards; check those answers against the recording too. The review record is your own method, not an automatic grade.


Use two passes to diagnose the next review. If your answer omits a stated condition, return to that condition and try again; if it adds a claim the lecture never makes, remove it and locate the actual supporting passage. If the audio itself is unclear, mark the source as unresolved before judging your answer. Recheck only that question after revisiting the relevant passage, then keep the earlier attempt beside the correction.

## Example: a fictional lecture on organizing a workshop

Imagine a lecturer explains that a materials list can reveal missing supplies but does not establish whether those supplies work. This is an invented teaching example, not a real lecture or app test.

- Question: What does the list help reveal? Your answer should retain the stated purpose: missing supplies.
- Follow-up: What does the list not establish? The stated limit is whether supplies work. Check the actual source before recording a similar answer for a real lecture.
- Review decision: If you remembered the benefit but missed the limit, revisit both passages together. Do not turn the example into a claim that a list prevents every preparation problem.

For your own lecture, retain the source link and enough surrounding words to find the explanation again. Use quotation marks only for exact speech checked against the recording. If the lecturer points to a diagram without explaining it aloud, read that diagram yourself and label it as a visual note.
