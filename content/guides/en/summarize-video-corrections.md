To summarize a video when the speaker corrects an earlier claim, create a short summary in Damas Insight Studio with the correction as its focus. Then check which earlier statement the speaker replaces, what the replacement says and which surrounding information remains unchanged. Write the final account from those checked passages. Reporting a correction faithfully does not independently prove that the replacement is true.

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use material you are authorized to process. Private, restricted and live videos are unsupported, and public sources can also be inaccessible. ## Establish what the correction actually replaces

Treat an earlier claim as replaced only when the recording explicitly connects the correction to that claim. A later sentence on the same topic may add a condition, describe another case or remain in conflict. If you cannot establish the link, keep both passages and mark their relationship unresolved; do not choose the later wording just because it comes last.

Use an optional record in your own notes with four fields:

- **Earlier statement:** the checked wording and its original scope.
- **Correction:** the passage that explicitly withdraws or revises it.
- **Replacement:** the new wording, including conditions and uncertainty.
- **Unchanged information:** surrounding instructions or claims that the correction does not withdraw.

Split the original statement into the part corrected and any part left intact. Record the earlier wording, the explicit correction, the replacement and the affected scope in your own note. Cross out only the superseded part in that working note, leaving the original transcript available for context. A correction to an identifier does not also withdraw an unchanged instruction about recording a storage location.

You build this record yourself from the source. If a crucial word is inaudible, hold the affected conclusion rather than using the generated summary to fill the gap.

## Fictional example: an object number and a shelf code

Imagine a training video about a catalog exercise. The presenter first says catalog numbers identify shelves. Later, they explicitly retract that statement: within this exercise, each object has a catalog number, while shelf codes describe storage locations. An instruction to record each object's storage location remains unchanged. This is an invented teaching example, not a real quotation, catalog standard or app test.

Your record would retain these distinctions:

- **Withdrawn:** the claim that catalog numbers identify shelves.
- **Replacement:** catalog numbers identify individual objects within the exercise.
- **Separate information:** shelf codes describe storage locations.
- **Still applicable:** record the object's storage location; the correction did not withdraw that instruction.

For a summary of the final teaching, a suitable paraphrase is: in the exercise, catalog numbers identify objects and shelf codes describe their storage locations; the presenter still instructs viewers to record the location.

If the reader needs the correction history, explain that the presenter initially assigned catalog numbers to shelves and then explicitly corrected that account. Keep the earlier claim visibly attributed to the withdrawn version. Do not list both versions as current facts.

If a real recording merely shows an object label later without correcting the earlier shelf statement, that visual difference alone does not establish a withdrawal. Inspect the demonstration, label your visual observation separately and leave the relationship open.

## Choose what your reader needs next

Decide whether your summary should explain the final account or the correction itself. For the final account, retain the replacement with its scope. For the correction history, retain both passages and explain their stated relationship. In either case, check that unaffected information survives and that you have not supplied a reason for the correction that the speaker never gives.

Keep the source link, checked passages and unresolved questions with your notes. Use quotation marks only for exact words checked against the recording; label shorter wording as your paraphrase. Never invent source facts, quotations, owners or deadlines. If the replacement matters to a consequential decision, seek separate primary evidence for its truth.
