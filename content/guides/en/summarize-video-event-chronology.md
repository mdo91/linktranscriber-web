To summarize events in the order they happened, start with a Damas transcript and built-in summary, then manually separate the speaker’s narration order from the event order they describe. Record the evidence for each sequence and leave uncertain relationships open instead of inventing dates or causes.

Damas accepts supported public, on-demand YouTube, Instagram and X video links up to 90 minutes. Use material you are authorized to process. Private, restricted and live sources are unsupported, and a public link can still be inaccessible. Damas is available on compatible iPhone, iPad and Mac devices.

## Prepare a source and an overview

1. Keep the original link and title. Choose the events and question you want your chronology to cover.
2. With an internet connection, sign in with Apple, provide the public link in Damas and review the AI processing consent. Check media-minute and Insight balances, usage history and processing cost before confirming. Available plans and optional top-up packs appear in the app.
3. Follow the app’s transcription and summary instructions. Choose a short or detailed Standard or Professional summary, adding your topic or context and preferred output language if useful. Keep the original transcript and the summary separately.
4. Processing may continue when you leave the app; a completion notification is available when notifications are enabled. Return to the full transcript and replay the relevant passages, checking dates, negatives and qualifications.
5. Watch any timeline or caption in the video yourself and label screen-only information separately from spoken evidence. Edit and copy the checked text into your notes.

The [general summary guide](/guides/summarize-video-transcript-iphone/) explains how to make an overview. An overview may follow the order in which the speaker tells the story, which is not necessarily the order of events.

## Build the sequence from explicit relationships

Make a separate record for each event: a brief paraphrase, the checked passage, any date or relative time stated, and the event it is explicitly before or after. Draw an arrow only where the source supports that relationship. If the speaker says two things happened before a launch without ordering them relative to each other, keep both before the launch but leave their mutual order unresolved.

Keep a stated date distinct from the video’s publication date and from the date you watched it. A date in the post does not establish when an event happened. An unexplained gap stays a gap; a later event does not by itself prove that the earlier event caused it. This is your manual chronology, not an automatic Damas verification feature.

## Fictional example: a project told out of order

Imagine a speaker opens with a project launch, then says a draft was revised after a trial and before launch. The recording does not say when the draft was first written or give calendar dates. This is an invented teaching example, not a real quotation or a test of the app.

| What the speaker describes | Supported relationship | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trial | Before the revision | The trial’s date |
| Revision | After the trial and before launch | When the draft was first written |
| Launch | After the revision | Whether the trial caused the changes |

A suitable summary is: the trial preceded a revision, and the revised project was then launched. Do not add a launch date or say the trial caused the revision unless the source establishes it. If the passage is inaudible, keep the relevant arrow pending rather than completing the sequence from context.

## Copy the chronology with its limits

Retain the source link, each supporting passage and unresolved relationships with your summary. Label paraphrases as your own and use quotation marks only for exact words checked against the recording. A faithful account of what a speaker says does not independently establish that the events happened as described.

Use [quotation checking](/guides/verify-interview-quotes/) for exact words and [source-linked notes](/guides/share-checked-video-notes/) when sharing. No external LLM is needed for this manual method. Processing time and accuracy vary; if a source becomes inaccessible, leave unfinished checks open. Share only within your rights.
